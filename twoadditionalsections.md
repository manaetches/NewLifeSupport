1. YOUR PRIVACY MATTERS TO US
At New Life Support Agency, we know that the information you share with us is personal, and often deeply private. We treat it with care and respect, and we protect it in line with the Privacy Act 2020, the Health Information Privacy Code 2020, and the Code of Health and Disability Services Consumers’ Rights.
This page explains what information we collect, why we need it, how we keep it safe, and your rights.
What information we collect
To provide safe, quality support, we may collect:
Contact details: your name, address, phone number, email and emergency contacts
Health and disability information: such as diagnoses, medications and care needs
Assessments and support plans
Cultural and communication preferences, so we can support you in a way that’s right for you
Financial information, only where it relates to funding your services
Records of any incidents or complaints
How we use your information
We only use your information for clear and necessary purposes:
Delivering your support: planning, coordinating and providing services that are safe and suited to you
Keeping everyone safe: protecting you and our team, and responding in an emergency
Improving our services: reviewing outcomes so we keep getting better, using de-identified information wherever possible
Reporting to funders: meeting our obligations to Health New Zealand and other funders
Meeting legal requirements: where we’re required to by law or a court order
Who we share your information with
Your information stays confidential. We will never share or sell it for commercial purposes.
We only share your information with your consent, unless the law requires it or someone’s safety is at risk.
If we need to share information with other health providers, we’ll ask for your consent first and record it.
Our team members can only see the information they need to do their job.
We’ll only share your information with your whānau if you’ve said that’s okay. The exception is when you are unable to make that decision yourself.
How we keep your information safe
Electronic records are stored in a secure care management system. Access depends on each person’s role.
Paper records are kept in locked cabinets that only authorised staff can open.
When records are no longer needed, we dispose of them securely. Paper is shredded and electronic files are permanently deleted.
Our staff will never photograph you or your records without your clear permission.
If something goes wrong
We take privacy breaches seriously. A breach is any time personal information is accessed, shared or lost without authorisation, whether by accident or on purpose.
If a breach happens, our team reports it straight away and we record and investigate it. If a breach causes, or is likely to cause, serious harm, we will tell you and notify the Privacy Commissioner, as the law requires.
Your rights
You’re in control of your information. You have the right to:
See the information we hold about you
Ask us to correct anything that’s inaccurate
To make a request, contact our office. We’ll respond within 20 working days.
2. KEEPING CHILDREN SAFE
At New Life Support Agency, we provide support in people’s homes, and children are often part of those homes and families. Every child deserves to be safe, cared for and free from harm. We are committed to protecting the safety and wellbeing of every child and young person we come into contact with. This includes children we support directly and children in the families of the adults we support.
Our child protection policy meets the requirements of the Children’s Act 2014 and the Oranga Tamariki Act 1989.
Our commitment
Child safety is everyone’s responsibility. Every member of our team is accountable for recognising and responding to concerns about a child’s safety.
Dedicated leadership: a designated child protection lead oversees our policy, coordinates our response to any concern, and supports staff who raise one.
Clear policies and procedures: our senior leaders make sure our child protection practices meet legal requirements and best practice, and we audit and review them regularly.
Safe recruitment
Everyone who joins our team goes through thorough safety checks before they start. These checks follow the requirements of the Children’s Act 2014 and the Children’s (Requirements for Safety Checks of Children’s Workers) Regulations 2015. They include identity verification, reference checks and Police vetting.
Trained and aware staff
All staff learn about child protection during induction, and we provide ongoing training. Our team is trained to:
Recognise the signs of abuse and neglect, including physical, emotional and sexual abuse and neglect of a child’s basic, medical, emotional or educational needs
Respond safely and appropriately when they have a concern
Know who to go to for advice and support
How we respond to concerns
If a staff member is worried about a child’s safety, we act promptly and carefully:
Safety comes first. We make sure the child is safe from any immediate danger.
We seek advice. Staff raise the concern straight away with our child protection lead or senior leadership.
We record it accurately. Concerns are documented clearly, factually and confidentially.
We involve the experts. Where needed, we report concerns to Oranga Tamariki or the New Zealand Police so that a child gets the right protection as quickly as possible.
Confidentiality and information sharing
We treat information about children and families with care and respect. We only share information when it’s needed to keep a child safe. The law, including the Privacy Act 2020, the Health Information Privacy Code 2020 and the Oranga Tamariki Act 1989, allows us to share information with Oranga Tamariki or the Police when we’re concerned about a child’s safety.
Our ongoing commitment
We review our child protection policy at least every three years, and sooner if the law or our practices change. This keeps our approach up to date and effective.
